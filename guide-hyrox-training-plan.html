HYROX guide

HYROX training plan.

A simple 12 week plan to get you to the start line fit, confident and ready to race.

By the M2 Training Club team · 29 September 2026

HYROX is eight 1km runs, each followed by a workout station. That means you need two things: a good running engine and the strength to keep moving through sleds, lunges and wall balls when your legs are already tired. A good HYROX training plan builds both, then teaches you to put them together.

This 12 week plan is written for someone racing HYROX Auckland on 4 to 7 February 2027, so week 1 starts around mid November. It works for any race date though. Just count back 12 weeks.

Before you start

This plan assumes you can already run 5km without stopping and you're training a few times a week. If you're not there yet, spend a few weeks on our running for beginners guide first. It will make the next 12 weeks far more enjoyable.

It also helps to know what you're training for. If you haven't looked at the race format yet, read HYROX stations explained for the eight stations in order.

The 12 weeks at a glance

  • Weeks 1 to 4, base. Mostly easy running, general strength and learning the stations. Keep the intensity low and the habits strong.
  • Weeks 5 to 8, build. Add running intervals, heavier station work and your first sessions that mix running with stations.
  • Weeks 9 to 11, race specific. Longer mixed sessions at your goal race pace. Practise transitions, pacing and fuelling.
  • Week 12, taper. Less training, a bit of sharpness, plenty of rest. Arrive fresh.

Make weeks 4 and 8 a little easier, with around a third less training. These lighter weeks let your body catch up so you can keep progressing.

A sample training week

Here's what a week in the build phase might look like. Move the days around to suit your life, but try not to put two hard days back to back.

  • Monday, strength. Squats, deadlifts, lunges, rows and carries. Moderate weight, good technique.
  • Tuesday, run intervals. For example 5 x 1km at a little faster than your goal race pace, with 90 seconds easy between.
  • Wednesday, stations. Technique and volume on the SkiErg, rower, sled, burpee broad jumps and wall balls.
  • Thursday, easy engine. An easy run or 30 to 45 minutes steady on the rower, bike or SkiErg.
  • Friday, rest. Walk, stretch, sleep.
  • Saturday, mixed session. Running and stations together, like 4 rounds of 1km run then one station.
  • Sunday, long easy run. Start around 40 minutes and build slowly towards 60 to 75 minutes.

Only have three days? Keep Tuesday, Saturday and a strength session that finishes with some station work. You'll still make good progress.

How to combine running and stations

The hardest part of HYROX isn't any one station. It's running well straight after one. Your legs feel heavy for the first few hundred metres, and many people go out too fast off the sled and pay for it later. Training this on purpose makes a big difference.

Once you're into the build phase, include at least one session a week where you run straight after station work. A few ways to do it:

  • Run 1km, do a station, repeat. Start with 3 or 4 rounds.
  • Pair a leg-heavy station with a run, like sled push or lunges into 500m to 1km of running.
  • Finish a strength session with 2 or 3 short runs at race pace.

Aim to run the first few hundred metres after a station a little easier, then settle into your pace. You want every 1km to look about the same, not fast early and slow late.

Race specific sessions

From week 9, make your mixed session longer and closer to the real thing. A half simulation is ideal. That's four runs and four stations at your goal race pace, either the first half of the race or the second half. The second half, with farmers carry, lunges and wall balls, is usually where people struggle, so practise it.

These sessions are also a good time to rehearse race day details. Wear the shoes and clothes you'll race in. Eat the same breakfast. Practise walking straight into each station without wasting time.

Keep your last big mixed session around 10 days out from race day. After that, you're not getting fitter, just more tired.

Keep strength in the plan

Sleds, lunges, carries and wall balls all reward strong legs, a strong back and a good grip. Keep one or two strength sessions every week right through the plan. You don't need to lift very heavy. Squats, deadlifts, lunges, rows, carries and some core work, done well and consistently, will carry over to race day. Our strength training 101 guide covers the basics.

The taper

In the final week, cut your training volume by about half. Keep a couple of short sessions with a bit of race pace work so your body stays switched on. A short run with a few quick efforts and some light station practice a few days out is plenty.

Use the extra time to sleep well, eat well and sort your race day logistics. Our first HYROX guide has a simple race day timeline if it's your first time.

Listen to your body

Twelve weeks is long enough for life to get in the way. If you miss a few days, just pick up where you left off. Don't try to cram missed sessions into one week. If something hurts in a sharp or lasting way, back off and get it checked by a physio. And if you have a health condition or haven't trained in a long time, check in with your doctor before you start.

Racing with a partner or a team? The same plan works, with a few extra things to practise together. See our HYROX Doubles and Relay tips.

Common questions

How many days a week should I train for HYROX?
Four to five sessions a week works well for most people: two or three runs, one or two strength sessions and one session that mixes running with stations. If you can only manage three, make one a run, one strength and one mixed session.
When should I start training for HYROX Auckland?
For HYROX Auckland on 4 to 7 February 2027, a 12 week plan starts around mid November 2026. If you are new to running or training, give yourself a few extra weeks to build a base first.
Should I do a full HYROX simulation before race day?
You don't need a full simulation. Half simulations of four runs and four stations teach you pacing and transitions without leaving you wrecked for days. Keep your last big mixed session about 10 days out from the race.
Do I need to taper for HYROX?
Yes. In the last week, cut your training volume roughly in half but keep a little intensity so you feel sharp. You won't get fitter in the final days, but you can arrive tired if you keep training hard.
