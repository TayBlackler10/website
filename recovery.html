The Science

Why Recovery Matters

🧊
Cold Water Immersion

Cold water causes your blood vessels to constrict and then dilate, flushing out the stuff that makes you sore. Regular cold exposure has been shown to reduce next-day soreness by up to 20%, which matters a lot when you're training multiple days a week.

🔥
Heat Therapy

Heat increases blood flow to muscles and improves sleep quality, both of which directly affect how well you recover and perform. Regular sauna use also does good things for cardiovascular health and stress levels.

🔄
Contrast Therapy

Going back and forth between the ice bath and sauna forces your blood vessels to rapidly open and close, creating a pumping effect that speeds up muscle repair. It's a protocol used by serious athletes for a reason.

💧
Active Recovery Swimming

Easy swimming is one of the best active recovery tools there is. The water pressure reduces swelling, and the gentle movement keeps circulation going and joints mobile without beating you up.

Getting the Most From Recovery

Recovery Protocol

1
Post-Training (0–30 min)

Get in the ice bath within 30 minutes of finishing your session, while inflammation is highest. Even 5 minutes makes a real difference to how you feel the next day.

2
Contrast Protocol

3 min ice bath → 10 min sauna. Repeat 2–3 rounds. Finish cold for recovery, warm for relaxation. Listen to your body never exceed comfortable limits.

3
Consistency is Key

Two or three recovery sessions a week makes a noticeable difference to performance and injury risk within a month or two. Treat it like training, not an optional extra.

4
Active Recovery Days

Use the 20m pool for easy swims on rest days 20–30 mins of easy freestyle maintains blood flow and mobility without adding to training load.
