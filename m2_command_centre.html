📖 Meta Ads Playbook M2 Training Club
What to do in every situation. Based on your actual ad account data and M2's business model.
How to read your ad signals
✅ GREEN Scale it
CPV under $0.40 · CTR above 1.5% · Frequency under 2.5× · CPM under $6 · Steady or rising LPVs
⚠️ YELLOW Watch & tweak
CPV $0.40–$0.65 · CTR 0.8–1.5% · Frequency 2.5–3.5× · CPM $6–10 · Flat or small dip in LPVs
🔴 RED Act now
CPV above $0.65 · CTR below 0.8% · Frequency above 3.5× · CPM above $10 · LPVs falling week-on-week
Your 3 campaigns what the numbers tell you
3 DAY TRIAL Hot · $1,441/mo $0.59/view · $81/day budget
This is your most important campaign. It drives the most trial volume and is your primary growth lever. CPV of $0.59 is in the yellow zone it was probably under $0.40 when creative was fresh.
When it's performing (CPV <$0.50)
Increase budget by 15–20% every 3–4 days. Don't jump more than 20% at once or Meta resets learning. Target is $120–150/day. Keep running the best-performing creative without touching it.
When it's flagging (CPV >$0.65)
New creative is the fix, not more budget. Shoot 2–3 new videos this week. Try: facility walkthrough, member result story, a day-in-the-life. Keep the same hook format if one worked. Duplicate the campaign with new creative before pausing the old one.
$45 A WEEK Active · $591/mo $0.26/view best CPV · $33/day budget
Your most efficient campaign. $0.26/view is excellent well into green territory. This targets people who already know they want a gym; they just need to pick one. Likely driven by a specific creative that's landing well.
Right now scale it up
This should be your second biggest budget. Push to $60–80/day. Your best CPV means your best dollar-in, member-out ratio here. Find which specific ad is driving the $0.26 CPV and put most of the budget behind that one.
If CPV climbs above $0.45
Frequency is probably the culprit your audience has seen it too many times. Broaden targeting (increase radius or age range), or launch a new creative variant. Keep the winner running alongside new tests.
PT COURSE Needs attention · $408/mo $0.65/view borderline red · $23/day budget
Your highest-ticket campaign ($4,000 course) but the creative is tired. $0.65/view is at the red boundary. The offer is strong the creative isn't doing it justice. One sale pays for 6 months of ads, so this is worth fixing.
What works for PT Course ads
Film a 30–60s "day in the life of an M2 PT" video. Show the income, the lifestyle, the community. A testimonial from a recent graduate is gold. Hook: "How I went from training myself to training 20 clients in 6 months." Target: 20–35, fitness-interested, not personal trainers.
Action this week
Pause or reduce budget to $10/day while you shoot new creative. Don't kill the campaign losing the learning data hurts. New creative test: 3 ad variants minimum. Brief: outcome-driven, real person, specific number (e.g. "earn $1,000/month within 90 days").
Your weekly Meta routine 3 things, every week
MONDAY Check the signals
Open the Campaigns tab. Check CPV for each campaign. Green → leave it. Yellow → note it. Red → plan new creative. Check frequency if any campaign is above 3×, new creative is urgent this week.
WEDNESDAY Creative review
Open Creative/Ads tab. Find the top 2 ads by video views and lowest CPV. These are your winners protect them. Find the bottom 2 by CPV or engagement these are candidates to pause or replace.
FRIDAY Budget & results
Check trials this week in Trial Analytics. Divide ad spend by trial sign-ups = your actual cost per trial. Target is under $15. If above $20, something isn't working. Check where trials are coming from in the member source data.

January 2026 $73,690 loss. What happened?
OPEX hit $162,876 nearly double any other month. The culprit: $63,312 Interest Expense (likely a loan refinance or penalty), $50,135 Shareholder Salaries, and $46,957 Rent. These three alone = $160K. Without these one-offs, January would have been a ~$10K profit month. This is not a recurring issue but needs to be understood and planned for.
† March 2026 is your end of financial year numbers are adjusted, not operational
March is M2's EOFY, so the accountant brought a full year of adjustments forward into that month: $172,000 rent (vs ~$50K normal), $50,135 shareholder salaries, $9,663 depreciation, plus reversing entries that show as negative interest and negative wages. None of that reflects what the gym actually spent running in March. Read March as a year-end true-up, not a trading month. The real operating picture either side of it (Feb, Apr, May) is the trend to watch.

Priority 1 Rent is the business. You need $180K/month revenue to make it work.
At $56K rent with your current cost structure, you need ~$180K revenue to hit a 10% net margin. That's 4,000 members at $45/wk or you need rent renegotiated. Every 10 new Perform members = +$1,950/mo revenue. You need 385 more Premium members to reach that revenue level.
Priority 2 Power bills are out of control ($16K in Sep, $12K in Mar)
These spikes are massive September was $16,440. Get an energy audit done. Pool, spa, sauna and ice bath are your biggest power draws. Look at off-peak heating, solar quotes, and a smart thermostat system. Even saving $3K/month is $36K/year straight to profit.
Priority 3 Towel hire at $2,793/month ($33,516/year)
That's a lot. Get a quote on buying your own towel stock and running in-house laundry. Could save $15–20K/year. Worth investigating once cash flow stabilises.
Priority 4 Four profitable months in a row keep the streak alive.
$14,209 net profit in July on rent of just $34,783 (31.2% of revenue) and wages at 28.5%. April, May, June and July have all now cleared a profit, with rent down sharply from June. July is the best month of 2026 on both revenue and profit. Your job is to make profitability the rule, not the exception hold revenue, keep wages near target, and keep pressure on the rent conversation.
You only need to add ~10 Perform members to break even consistently
Your average monthly result over the trailing 12 months (Aug 2025–Jul 2026) is now a $1,888 loss, a big improvement on the earlier $39.9K-window loss and dragged down almost entirely by January's one-off $73K hit. At $45/week per Perform member that's roughly 10 extra members converting from Daily or new joins to reach consistent break-even. Strip out January and the trailing run is firmly profitable April, May, June and July back to back prove the trajectory.

Passport visits have grown 167% since January 2025 but revenue per member is low
1,045 visits in Jan 25 to 2,787 in May 26. Income grew from $8,178 to $21,819 per month, a new record. But each Passport member generates an average of $24/month vs $108 for a Daily member and $195 for a Perform member. Passport is great passive income but should not replace owned memberships. Use Passport members to fill off-peak time slots and keep your floor busy without displacing full-paying members.
Passport income is variable no visit means no pay
If Passport members stop coming in school holidays, winter, a bad month your income drops with no warning. January 2026 dropped to 2,177 visits vs October 2025's 2,743. That's a $4,429 swing in a single month. Do not rely on Passport for more than 20% of total revenue without a contingency plan.
